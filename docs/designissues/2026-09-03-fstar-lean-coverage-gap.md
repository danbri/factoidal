# F\* test coverage versus verified Lean 4 coverage, measured

Date: 2026-09-03. Owner question: "the gap between our F\* test coverage
vs what we have working and verified in Lean 4 with no gaps/sorries/etc".

There are two axes. They are reported apart, because a suite that passes
and a suite that passes through total, proved code are different claims.

## The two-axis summary

**Axis 1 — conformance.** The Lean tree declares 31 probe targets
(`tools/internal-tests.sh --list`); 27 of them run an external suite.
The other 4 are a property-based generator, an F\*-versus-Lean
differential, an RDFS closure demo and one superseded XML probe.
On the owner's priority core the Lean tree is level with F\* or ahead:

- 📊 Level: SPARQL 1.1 (631 pass, 0 fail, out of 631), SPARQL 1.2
  (254 pass, 0 fail, out of 254), RDFC-1.0 (86 pass, 0 fail, out of 86),
  SHACL Core (98 pass, 0 fail, out of 98), SHACL SPARQL (22 pass, 0 fail,
  out of 22), SHACL 1.2 rules (88 pass, 0 fail, out of 88), ShEx
  (1182 pass, 0 fail, out of 1182), JSON-LD (467 and 791 of 793),
  CSVW csv2rdf and csv2json (270 pass, 0 fail, out of 270 each),
  JSON Schema (770 pass, 0 fail, out of 770), Schematron (8 pass, 0 fail,
  out of 8).
- ✅ Ahead: RDF 1.1 (Lean 1031 pass, 0 fail, out of 1031; F\* 1030 pass,
  0 fail, 1 unsupported, out of 1031). XML conformance (Lean scores 1862
  entries; F\* skips 1138 of 2585). GRDDL (both red, Lean less red).
- 🔴 Behind: **OWL is the one large gap** — F\* is near-perfect per
  catalog, Lean scores 1131 pass, 316 fail (out of 1457) in RL mode.
  Then SHACL 1.2 core and SPARQL (34 fail), RIF Core, XSLT, RML-Core,
  and the VC/DID stack.
- ⚪ No Lean runner at all: CSVW validation, RML-io, DID, GeoSPARQL,
  XForms, QUDT, MathML presentation, XPath as a suite of its own.

**Axis 2 — soundness of the Lean side.** ✅ **Zero `sorry`, zero user
`axiom`, zero `native_decide`, zero `unsafe`, zero `@[implemented_by]`.**
A full `lake build` is green and its 817 captured `#print axioms` outputs
name only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

⚠️ The escape hatch that is not zero is **`partial def`: 217
declarations across 43 files**. It is not spread evenly. RDF, RDFS,
SPARQL, SHACL, JSON-LD, Cottas, HDT, VC and Crypto contain **none**.
Every one of the 217 sits in ShEx (50), XPath (39), OWL (32), XSLT (27),
Harness (16), RIF (15), Math (11), Geo (6), MathML (5), Testing (4),
GRDDL (3), and single digits elsewhere.

**So the two axes give the same answer from opposite ends.** The areas
where Lean matches F\* on conformance AND carries no `partial def` are
RDF, SPARQL, SHACL, JSON-LD and RDFC-1.0. The areas where Lean is behind
on conformance are, with the exception of RML and CSVW validation, the
same areas that carry the `partial def` count. A `partial def` compiles
to an opaque constant, so no theorem can be stated about the function it
declares. ShEx passes 1182 of 1182 through 50 of them.

FIXED 2026-09-03. The id was `dt-rng`, a prefix of `dt-rng-intersect` at
`docs/theorem-registry.md:2631`, which is not an OWL 2 RL rule id: the
regex ended at a `-`, because `-` is a non-word character. The same

`prp-inv`, `prp-rfl`) entered the same way. This paragraph first
estimated the corrected figure as "55 rule ids, 50 named"; that was
arithmetic on the broken set, not a measurement.

The tool now reads whole hyphenated tokens, keeps only the 78 rule ids
the OWL 2 Profiles Recommendation defines, prints every token it
dropped, and matches Lean theorem names by camel/snake segment rather
than by substring. The `dt-branch` name-specific exclusion is deleted.
The measured figure is **52 rule ids, 47 named by a Lean theorem, 5
absent by agreement with the F\* side, 0 real gaps.**

**What a `partial def` costs.** It compiles to an opaque constant with no
equation lemmas and no kernel reduction, so it is invisible to `decide`
and cannot be the subject of an equation proof. It is NOT invisible to
`#guard`, which evaluates through the interpreter — measured 2026-08-26
and recorded in `skills/factoidal-lean-basics`. So these functions can be
pinned by example and cannot carry a theorem. Tracked at
<https://github.com/danbri/factoidal/issues/617>.

The count moved from the 226 recorded on 2026-08-29 in
`skills/factoidal-lean-basics` to 217 today. Two commits account for ten
of the eleven library retirements: `d0ca92db7` (five XPath/Mini partials
given structural termination) and `887551bf1` (five OWL.ClassExpr
partials given termination measures). The remaining one is unattributed;
the harness side rose by two, also unattributed.

## The ranked gap list

Ordered by the owner's priority, 2026-08-24 verbatim: "core of rdf/s,
sparql, owl, rif, csvw, shacl, shex, xml, xpath, cslt, schematron are
more urgent" than COTTAS. "cslt" is read as XSLT.

1. 🔴 **OWL 2 conformance.** The single largest gap on the whole board.
   F\* is near-perfect on every catalog it runs; the Lean probe scores
   1131 pass, 316 fail (out of 1457) with the RL closure and 1193 pass,
   254 fail with the DL tableau. `type-inconsistency.rdf` alone is 30
   pass, 97 fail (out of 128) in RL mode against an F\* line of 126 pass,
   1 fail (out of 127). The tableau ladder is the named route:
   <https://github.com/danbri/factoidal/issues/548>. The `--dl` wall of
   4 m 30 s is a separate item,
   <https://github.com/danbri/factoidal/issues/549>. This work also sits
   behind 32 `partial def` declarations in `OWL/Refute.lean` and
   `OWL/Materialise.lean`, so it is an axis-1 and an axis-2 item at once.
2. 🔴 **RIF Core.** Lean decides 26 of 50 entries and fails 2 of them;
   F\* scores 46 pass, 0 fail, 4 skip (out of 50). The 13 undecided
   entries are the RIF-DTB built-ins the Lean library does not carry.
3. 🔴 **CSVW validation.** F\* scores 281 pass, 1 fail (out of 282); the
   Lean tree has csv2rdf and csv2json runners at full parity but no
   validation runner. This is the cheapest priority-core gap on the
   list, because the validator that scores the 58 negative CSVW entries
   already exists inside both Lean CSVW runners.
4. 🔴 **SHACL 1.2 core and SPARQL.** Lean reads 160 entries from the 1.2
   manifest and fails 34; F\* scores 138 pass, 0 fail and 25 pass, 0 fail
   on its two selections. SHACL 1.0 and SHACL 1.2 rules are already at
   parity, so this is a bounded piece of work, and it carries no
   `partial def`.
5. 🟡 **ShEx, XSLT, XPath — an axis-2 gap, not an axis-1 one.** ShEx is at
   full parity (1182 pass, 0 fail, out of 1182) and XSLT is 3 failures
   short. What is missing is not tests: it is that 50 + 39 + 27 = 116
   `partial def` declarations mean no theorem can be stated about any of
   them. Under the founding view — the specs are what make aggressive
   variation safe — these three areas currently have conformance
   evidence and no proof surface.
6. 🟡 **XML.** Lean scores 1862 entries where F\* skips 1138 of 2585, so
   the Lean side is ahead on reach and carries 22 failures. The gap is
   the 59 namespace cases the Lean profile declares out of scope, which
   F\* covers through `XML.Namespaces.fst`.
7. 🟡 **RML-Core and RML-io.** Lean compares 60 of the 76 RML-Core
   entries and does not attempt the 15 negative ones; RML-io has no Lean
   runner. RML is not on the owner's named priority list.
8. 🟡 **The VC and DID stack.** F\* runs 215 entries across four suites;
   Lean runs 71. Not on the priority list.
9. 🟡 **JSON-LD framing.** 🔴 in BOTH trees — F\* 28 pass, 64 fail and
   Lean 29 pass, 63 fail (out of 92) — and absent from `latest.json`, so
   the public dashboard does not show it. This is a shared defect, not a
   port gap.
10. ⚪ **GeoSPARQL, XForms, QUDT, MathML presentation, the Xalan XSLT
    mirror, EECC interop.** No Lean runner. None is on the priority
    list, and the F\* evidence for GeoSPARQL, XForms and MathML is
    self-authored rather than upstream, so porting the runner would
    carry that weakness across rather than close a gap.
11. ⚪ **`Parquet.Footer` and the COTTAS columnar store.** The only F\*
    module the port-gap tool classes as engine code still to port is
    `Parquet.Footer` (3373 lines), and it is the module the owner ruled
    on: "DO NOT PORT THAT TERRIBLE HEX CRAP",
    <https://github.com/danbri/factoidal/issues/566>. The Lean tree does
    not lack a store — it has Shardborough, a different design, with a
    2026-09-03 executability census of 535 executed, 535 matched, 0
    differed (out of 535 eligible default-graph tests). Under "port
    functionality, not files", storage is covered by a different
    artifact, and COTTAS is last on the owner's order anyway.

1. **The module-coverage figure (202 of 220) cannot see a consolidation
   or a substantive rename.** It matches on the full F\* module name, the
   last two name parts, or a hand-written alias. If one Lean file carries
   the work of three F\* modules under a name resembling none of them,
   the tool reports three gaps that do not exist; if a Lean file carries
   a matching name and half the content, the tool reports coverage that
   does not exist. The alias table also cannot report its own omissions.
   This is exactly the failure that made the reported figure 120 of 220
   when the real one was 125 (hazard #28).
2. **Module coverage says nothing about how much of a module arrived.**
   `SPARQL11.Algebra` is aliased and counts as covered while
   `strip_rewrite_internal_vars` and two helpers were absent from the
   Lean tree. Rule 6b of `skills/counting-coverage`.
3. **`#print axioms` covers 817 declarations, not every declaration.**
   The 824 invocations are hand-placed, one per headline theorem. A
   theorem nobody wrote a `#print axioms` line for is not in the 817. The
   count is a project convention, not a completeness guarantee.
4. **FIXED 2026-09-03 — the Lean CI gate did not run an axiom audit.**
   `.github/workflows/verify-lean4.yml` ran `lake build L4Factoidal` and
   nothing else. A `sorryAx` breaks that build, because Lean emits it
   with an elaboration ERROR (the 2026-08-31 finding in
   `docs/20260831-lean-ci-gate.md`). A deliberate user `axiom` would NOT
   break it — it would compile, and its appearance in a `#print axioms`
   line is an informational message, not a failure. The workflow now
   runs `tools/lean-hygiene-audit.py --build-log …` as a second step. It
   fails on any `sorry`, user `axiom`, `native_decide`, `unsafe` or
   `@[implemented_by]`, on any `#print axioms` output in the build log
   naming an axiom other than `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
   `Quot.sound`, and on any INCREASE in the `partial def` count against
   `tools/lean-partial-def-baseline.txt` (217 today). It strips comments
   and string literals before counting, because a raw grep sees 227
   `partial def` of which 10 are prose.
5. **Grep cannot see through an import, and the two halves cover
   different things.** The grep counts in the axis-2 table are counts of
   DECLARATIONS in this tree. The `#print axioms` result is what the
   proofs DEPEND on. Neither alone is the answer; the table says which
   produced each figure.
6. **A `partial def` count is a count of declarations, not of risk.**
   Sixteen of the 217 are in `Harness/`, which is runner plumbing rather
   than library semantics. The table separates them; no weighting is
   claimed beyond that.
7. **RESOLVED 2026-09-03 — the two Lean runners cover different sets,
   and both numbers are right.** `l4w3c` reads `manifest.ttl`; its
   rdf-xml sub-score is 166 pass, 0 fail (out of 166), inside the 1031
   pass, 0 fail (out of 1031) total. `l4rdfxml-probe` walks the
   DIRECTORY on purpose, so that it measures the RDF/XML parser without
   also depending on the Turtle parser to read the manifest (its own
   header says so). The suite holds 173 `.rdf` files and 166 manifest
   entries: upstream comments out 7 of them
   (`third_party/testing/w3c/rdf/rdf11/rdf-xml/manifest.ttl` lines 56,
   59, 65, 166-169). The probe's 2 failures,
   `rdfms-xml-literal-namespaces/test001` and `test002`, are two of the
   7 withdrawn entries; the withdrawn entries' own comment gives the
   reason — treatment of namespaces that are not visibly used in an XML
   literal "is implementation dependent". Neither runner counts a skip
   as a pass. 1031 pass, 0 fail (out of 1031) is the conformance
   number; 130 pass, 2 fail (out of 132) is a parser probe over the
   directory including withdrawn entries. ⚠️ Still open: the probe's
   own report line does not SAY that, and changing it needs an edit to
   `formal/lean4/Harness/RdfXmlProbe.lean`. The rule is recorded as
   `skills/counting-coverage/SKILL.md` rule 9.
8. **Several rows compare different denominators over the same corpus.**
   RDF 1.2 (1363 against 371), OWL (per-type against per-case), XML
   conformance (1862 in profile against 1447 of 2585 with 1138 skips),
   SHACL 1.2 (160 read against 163 selected). A Lean denominator far
   below the F\* one means the runner did not READ those entries; a Lean
   denominator far ABOVE it means the F\* runner selected a subset. In
   neither direction is the ratio a pass rate.
